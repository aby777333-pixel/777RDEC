Describing a permissions model does not convince anyone. Being made to type a
confirmation before anything is permitted to act is a different experience.

**EMIL Lab** is the real control surface, running against simulated data. You
set the analysis permissions, the markets, the risk limits, the maximum
exposure, the automation level and the trading permissions. Then you request
arm, read what you are authorising, and type it.

## What we deliberately made awkward

- **Arming requires typing.** There is no one-click arm, because arming is an
  act of authorisation.
- **The authorisation text is specific.** It states what will be permitted, the
  hard limits, and that the intelligence layer cannot remove market risk.
- **The dialog does not close by clicking outside it.** Dismissing an
  authorisation prompt by accident should not be possible.

## What we deliberately made easy

- **Disarming.** One button, always rendered, high contrast, no confirmation.
  The Escape key works too. Withdrawing authorisation should never require
  navigating anywhere.

The log narrates what the layer is doing in readable language — including
refusals, which are the interesting part. An order rejected for breaching a
concentration limit tells you more about a system than one that filled.
